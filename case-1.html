The pay that stopped: Ilse's Airbnb and baby care work

Posted 9 October 2026 · Updated 11 October 2026 · Ilse's own story

Her story

Ilse lives on the Gold Coast. She moved there about three months before she wrote her Facebook post on 30 June 2025. She had worked as a nanny in Brisbane and in Vancouver, and she wanted to keep doing that work in her new home.

Valeria asked her to care for the couple's newborn when the family came back from the United States. While she waited for that to start, Ilse managed their Airbnb, working for Valeria and her husband Joshua.

Then her pay stopped. That was about a month and a half before her post. The reasons were bank problems, and a promise that she would be paid when the family was back in Australia. She stayed patient. She kept working.

When the family came back, the baby care job was cancelled with no warning. The baby was put in daycare instead. And the pay for the month and a half of work she had already done was refused.

Ilse and her boyfriend went to talk to them in person. They were told the couple were not happy with her work. That part still hurts, because Ilse has the messages from that time, praising her work day after day.

The messages talk about pay being pending while a bank account was verified, about the family being out of Australia and the trip back, about sorting the pay the next week, and about Joshua being able to pay, including paying cash.

Ilse reported it to Fair Work. At the time of her post, she was looking for a lawyer.

The details
  • When: the work started about three months before 30 June 2025. The pay stopped about a month and a half before that date.
  • Where: Gold Coast, Queensland.
  • In short: Airbnb management work for Valeria and Joshua, with baby care work promised after their return. The pay stopped, the baby care work was cancelled, and the pay for work already done was refused.
  • Who she told: Fair Work first. Then she took it to QCAT, and QCAT decided in her favour on 28 January 2026. The decision is below.

The QCAT decision

Ilse took the pay fight to QCAT, the Queensland Civil and Administrative Tribunal. After a hearing, the tribunal ordered Joshua J. Hackett and Valeria Hackett to pay her $3,377.90 within fourteen (14) days. The decision is dated 28 January 2026. Case number: Q10777-25.

Ilse also reported the matter to police.
