Why these stories are in one place

This page puts in one place the stories people shared after a Facebook warning post on 30 June 2025. Ilse, a woman on the Gold Coast, wrote that post about work she did for a couple: Valeria Hacket (also written Hackett) and Joshua Hacket. Her pay stopped, and the pay for the work she had already done was refused. She wrote the post so it would not happen to the next person.

Since then, more people have come forward with stories of their own about the same couple. An Airbnb guest. A dog breeder. A woman in Norway whose bank cards were used. A seller in Mexico who delivered a mattress and was never paid. Read the stories before you work for them, rent to them, stay with them, or sell to them.

The stories

  • 30 June 2025: The pay that stopped: Ilse's Airbnb and baby care work
    Ilse's own story. She managed the couple's Airbnb while she waited for baby care work to begin. Her pay stopped about a month and a half before her post. QCAT later ordered the couple to pay her $3,377.90. The decision paper is on her story page.
  • September 2020: The bank card in Norway: a fraud report from 2020
    A woman in Norway found her bank cards used for IKEA and PayPal purchases. 19,094 NOK was charged, and more was attempted. The Oslo police recorded a fraud report, and her lawyers prepared a civil lawsuit. Her documents are on her story page, with every name covered except Valeria's.
  • 29 April 2026: The mattress that was never paid for: a seller's story from Mexico
    A seller in the Riviera Veracruzana, Mexico, delivered a mattress and its base to a woman who said she would pay by transfer. The money never arrived, the excuses came, and then she blocked him. His story was also published by the Mexican news site La Nigua.
  • 30 June 2025: Cancelled hours before arrival: a guest's Airbnb stay
    A guest wrote this under the post. Their booking was cancelled hours before they arrived. Joshua was pretty threatening, they cut the stay short, and they lost a lot of money.
  • 1 July 2023: The rent, the receipt and the locksmith day: an apartment report
    A landlord rented their apartment to Valeria Hojem and Zaira Gallardo. The agreed rent was not paid, a transfer receipt was false, and after two weeks of rent the pair disappeared. The original post is shown on the story page.
  • September 2026: A puppy, a payment that failed: a breeder's story
    A registered breeder of 27 years let a puppy go before a RightPaw payment cleared. The payment failed, and her calls and messages were never answered. It is the first time anything like this has happened to her.
  • 18 October 2025: The deposit that never came back: an Airbnb guest's story
    A guest at the couple's Airbnb never got his deposit back. He had already had a hearing at GC Court when he wrote to Ilse in October 2025.

A 30 second game: spot the red flags

Four real moments from the stories on this page. Could you spot the warning signs? Tap an answer.

1. You delivered a mattress. The buyer says she has no cash and will transfer the money. Nothing shows up. She says her account is international and it will land in a few hours. Safest move?

This is exactly how the seller in Story 3 lost his mattress. A transfer that has not landed is not a payment.

2. A couple offers you work. Pay day keeps moving because of "bank problems". What is the safest move?

That is Ilse's story, Story 1. She kept working for a month and a half. QCAT later ordered the couple to pay her $3,377.90.

3. A buyer wants to take a puppy today and pay through RightPaw. The payment has not cleared yet. Safest move?

In Story 6, the payment failed two days after the puppy was collected, and the calls were never answered again.

4. Someone stops answering your messages right after getting your money or your goods. First step?

Screenshots are proof. The woman in Story 2 kept hers, and the Oslo police recorded her fraud report.
